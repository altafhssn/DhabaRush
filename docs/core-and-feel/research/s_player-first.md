# Dhaba Rush, player first: what the genre promises and where we keep or break it

1 Nov 2026. Lens: what the player feels, minute by minute. Tags (r1–r7, g1–g3, p0) name the research files in this folder by prefix. Facts about the game come only from p0. Verification corrections are applied. Reviews are paraphrased.

## (a) The core of the genre

The core is a machine you can watch that pays you, plus one decision at a time about where to put the money. Customers walk in, a timer fills, money flies up, and the player taps to buy the next thing. Three things must hold. First, something is always affordable early, and a new goal shows up every few minutes: costs grow faster than income, and threshold multipliers push income back ahead (r1 §2, Pecorella). Second, the player can see which link in the chain is slowest and fix it. That is the genre's only real decision (r1 §8; r4 §8.3; r5 on Idle Restaurant Tycoon). Third, the place visibly grows. When it is finished there is a next place that feels like a promotion, and money is waiting each time the player returns (r1 §8; r4 §8.7; g2 §6). Underneath all three sits calm: no fail state, no forced interruption, one thing to do on screen (r1 §8.6; r2 §7).

Decoration is everything that sells the theme without changing those three things: vehicle types, day and night, fairy lights, music, horns, the guide character, dish names, chest styling, the look of the VIP popup (r1 §8). For Indian players it matters more than usual, because recognition earns 5★ in local-theme games (r7 §2). It still cannot rescue a broken seesaw or an unreadable bottleneck. Juice is the same: in Juul and Begy's study, players rated the juicy build higher but played no better (r6 §6). Juice must never bury the NEXT chip or a timer.

## (b) What players look for, ranked

1. **Ads they choose, that always pay.** This is the genre's most repeated praise (Eatventure, four years running) and its most common reason for a 1★ review (r2 §1–2; r5). In g3's Play India sample, ads come up in 47% of 1–3★ reviews and 15% of 4–5★ ones.
2. **A game that opens, runs and stays cheap on their phone.** The top Hindi complaint is "won't start or loads slowly" (12.6% of 1–3★), then MB and data (7.0%). The corpus's most-helpful review praises offline play (g3). Heat and lag come up in 20–35% of Indian driving-sim reviews (r7 §1).
3. **Knowing their job.** With no goal named, "everything runs by itself" reads as confusion (g3, quote 1). Kolibri's soft-launch players got stuck at one tutorial step (r5). Dhaba's testers called it hard to understand (p0 §7).
4. **A calm screen with one next thing.** Players count popups: 25 in a row in Idle Miner, 27 closed in about three minutes in Idle Restaurant (r1 §5; g2 §3).
5. **A place they can watch grow, where upgrades change the picture** (r2 #4, #6; r4 §8.4). An upgrade with no visible change feels wasted (r6, the Burger Please stove).
6. **Respect for any session length, with money waiting.** Five minutes or an hour, without losing their place. Offline caps run 2–3 hours, and Idle Restaurant Tycoon's 1-hour cap is a named complaint (r2 #2; r5; g2 §6).
7. **Always a next place or event.** "Nothing left to do" and "I finished it, please update" are day-1 and week-1 reasons to quit. Long-tenure players name weekly events as what keeps them (r2 §3–4; g3, quote 30).
8. **Fair progress, no paywall.** In g3's sample, 20.7% of 1–3★ reviews mention price and 7.3% say pay-to-win. Idle Supermarket's English reviews average 1.77★ since its v4 manager cards (g3).
9. **Recognition and charm.** In Bus Simulator India, words like real, Indian and desi appear in 75% of 4–5★ reviews and 43% of 1–3★ (r7 §2). Players also praise humour in upgrade text (r2 #7).
10. **Something to think about, with honest numbers.** The optimizer minority wants the bottleneck named, the cost and payoff of each upgrade, and an honest per-minute income (r2 §5).

## (c) What they currently get, and the gaps

| Player want | What the market gives (Oct 2026) | Gap Dhaba Rush can fill |
|---|---|---|
| Optional ads | Eatventure, yes (4.7★ Play). Idle Supermarket, Pizza Ready, Roadside Empire, Truck Stop force ads; Pizza Ready players report one a minute (r3 §5; r5) | No forced ads early |
| Runs on their phone, offline | Downloads of 169–929 MB; "waiting for server" screens in Idle Supermarket and Idle Theme Park (r7 §1; r2; g2) | A small, offline build (inference, r7) |
| One next thing | Popup walls at launch in Idle Miner, Idle Restaurant, Travel Center (r3 §5; g2) | Dhaba's single NEXT chip |
| A next place | Every comp has cities or stages; the complaint becomes "same food in every city" (r3 §5; r5) | Regional menus per highway (r5; r7). Dhaba has none yet |
| No paywall | Idle Supermarket v4 gates upgrades behind manager cards ~16 min after the first move (g2) | Delegation earned in play |
| Recognition | No India-themed idle at scale: Chaiwala Tycoon 5K+ installs, Idle Indian Restaurant 10K+ (g3; r7) | Open, but demand unproven (r7 §4) |
| Seated service | Idle Restaurant's walkouts are hated; Pizza Ready's Nov 2025 staff-delivered tables drew no comment (g1) | Visible only if we show it |

## (d) Case study lessons

**Idle Supermarket Tycoon (r4, g2, corrections)**
1. The opening is the part that still works. A 2026 reviewer says the tutorial is fun and the rest is not (r4 §5). Copying the opening buys one session and nothing more.
2. Red bottleneck stats turn "what do I buy" into a small puzzle (r4 §8.3).
3. v4 moves the player to a second store 9–25 minutes in and shows the next store as a priced, locked card (g2 §1). The reset is a tutorial beat, not a surprise.
4. From Jan–Feb 2026, v4 put department upgrades behind manager cards sold in loot-box packs, and players read it as a paywall (g2; g3; verdict). Managers sold this way are a warning, not a model.
5. Dhaba's VIP ×2 popup with a free reward matches a v4 beat at about 2.5 minutes. The 2019–20 guides describe a different game (g2 §6).

**Eatventure, plus Kolibri's Idle Restaurant Tycoon (r5, g1, g2)**
1. The ad contract wins ratings. "No forced ads" is the top praise from 2022 to Oct 2026, and Eatventure out-rates Idle Supermarket on Play, 4.7 to 4.1 (r5 §5).
2. Little text and no big tutorial: the visuals tell the story (Lessmore's 2022 interview, r5).
3. A renovation preview appears at about 3:00, and the first renovation comes at 5:30–6:30. The first city still takes days (g2 §2). Finishing feels like a promotion, and nothing is lost.
4. A fixed weekly calendar, with a weekend headline event and a mid-week race, is a cadence a small team can run. Events feed the main game through permanent items (r5 §2).
5. Idle Restaurant Tycoon is the closest analogue to Dhaba's twist: tables, waiters, a door counter of vacant tables and an overview of where money comes from. Its walkouts with no fix and its pathing bugs drew the angriest reviews (g1 §2).

**Pizza Ready! (r6, r3, g1, corrections)**
1. Start at once: no main menu and no complicated tutorial. Arrows and pads do the teaching (r6; verdict).
2. Everything is physical: cash lies on the floor and the stacks are visible. An upgrade the player can't see is a wasted purchase (r6 §7–8).
3. The player feels the chore first, then a hire arrives as relief. In its sibling My Perfect Hotel, something new arrives every 1–3 minutes for the first 12 minutes (r6 §3).
4. Scale is not love. It has about 400M lifetime installs and was India's #4 game by downloads in 2025 (r3; verdict). Yet its reviews are dominated by ad density, including Hindi reviews of ads every few seconds, and by a content ceiling (r6 §5; g3).
5. Since Nov 2025, staff carry food to the tables, and no review in g1's sample mentions it (g1). That validates Dhaba's twist but makes it less new. It only matters if the player can see it.

**India's local-theme hits (r7, g3)**
1. Recognition earns stars: real roads, named trucks, horns (r7 §2).
2. Truck and bus sim players already ask, unprompted, for a dhaba stop (r7 §2).
3. Shallow authenticity is punished: only the vehicles feeling Indian, empty scenes, identical cities, Hindi-only boards (r7 §3).
4. Problems that break the session decide 1★ reviews: won't start, MB, data, heat. Offline play is a selling point (g3, quotes 19–23).
5. The business fantasy translates: Hindi reviewers call Pizza Ready a game for people in business, and creators' hits are titled "I opened my own…" (r7; g3). But Hindi idle-tycoon videos get tiny audiences, so theme alone won't find players (g3).

## (e) Dhaba Rush

**Core promise.** Run a busy highway dhaba that works by itself: lorries pull in, travellers eat on charpais, and money lands in the galla. You make it busier by spotting what is holding it back (seats, waiters, cooks or the galla) and fixing it with a tap or two.

**Feel targets as observable moments**, each checkable in a screen recording of a tester on their own phone. The first three minutes deliver. The feel breaks where the twist hides in panels, where a forced ad lands on a purchase, and where next steps run out.

**KEEP / FIX / ADD / CUT.** Priority: P1 = before 15 Dec, P2 = during soft launch, P3 = after.

KEEP
- K1 (P1) **The first-3-minute beat sheet**: sale at 0:37, upgrade ~0:45, hire ~1:10, each twist opened by a visible problem and closed in two taps. Smallest version: freeze it; change only what FIX names. r4 §10; r6 §8.
- K2 (P1) **The NEXT and PROBLEM chips, honest /min, before→after on HIRE.** Smallest version: as is. g3, implication 1; r2 §5; r4 §8.3.
- K3 (P1) **Seats and waiters as the bottleneck chain, whole orders on trays.** Smallest version: sim as is; FIX handles legibility. r1 §5; g1.
- K4 (P1) **Recognition.** Keep the lorries reversing into bays, the four Indian horns, charpais, the kettle on the bhatti, fairy lights and Devanagari boards. Smallest version: if they aren't there yet, add "Horn OK Please" to the first lorry and serve chai in small glasses. r7 §2, §6.
- K5 (P1) **The ad bubble as a gift.** FREE CASH in session 1; VIP appears only when the player taps. r2 #1; r4 §8.5; g3, implication 1.

FIX
- F1 (P1) **Interstitials.** Today they start in session 4 and fire 0.6 s after an upgrade. Smallest version: none within 60 s of a purchase, hire or reward. Never on WELCOME BACK or during a build beat. At most one every 3–5 minutes. Block rummy, real-money and dating ad categories. r6 §8 guardrail; g3, implication 2.
- F2 (P1) **Force the seats beat** for every player by injecting arrivals. g1 #7; p0 §8.
- F3 (P1) **Walkouts.** Today a door line of four gives up after 8 s, shown as LOST. Smallest version: make the door line lossless, with vehicles driving past when it is full. Or stretch the wait to 20–30 s with a ring over each head, and allow losses only when the fix is affordable. Show the cost as "+₹X/min with one more table". g1 #1.
- F4 (P1) **Hire where the problem is.** Tables already sell from a 2-tap card on the ghost. Smallest version: the same 2-tap HIRE card over the plates waiting on the pass; the new waiter lands there and takes the oldest plate. A new table seats the door line at once. g1 #6; r6 §8.1; p0 §9 #2.
- F5 (P1) **Put the twist on screen.** Smallest version: a painted board at the gate reading "Seats 6/8", a visible plate stack on the pass, and one "what's slowing us" line from the start rather than HISAAB after the kadai. g1 #2; r6 §8.4; p0 §9 #4.
- F6 (P1) **Rewarded ads always pay.** Smallest version: grant the reward if the ad fails. Hide the bubble when no ad is available or the phone is offline. Run the sim fully offline. g3, implication 3; r7, implication 2.
- F7 (P2) **The lever the tutorial teaches goes flat right after it.** Smallest version: when LEVEL UP isn't the limit, show its real /min gain and promote the bottleneck banner's button above it. p0 §9 #1; r1 §3 (Pecorella: if the newest generator always wins, the choices disappear); r6 (an upgrade you can't see is wasted).
- F8 (P2) **Price ladder.** Smallest version: Remove Ads priced below VIP (₹99–199) and a ₹10–49 starter, both labelled one-time and forever. No more offers after a purchase. r7, implication 4; g3, implication 4.

ADD
- A1 (P1) **A next step the player can see.** Smallest version: one renovation, "Dhaba → Family Dhaba with an AC hall", shown from about minute 3 as a locked card with a price and a before/after picture. It unlocks where new content now runs out (35–50 min), and the player chooses when. Levels reset; unlocked dishes and a permanent income multiplier carry over. Regional highways (Punjab, Rajasthan, the south) come later. g2 §6; r5 §5; r4 §8.7; r7, implication 7; r7 §6 (dhabas adding AC family halls).
- A2 (P2) **A daily reason to open.** Smallest version: a free crate on the first open each day, with fixed contents (minutes of takings) and no random draw. g2 §6.
- A3 (P2) **Life in the scene.** Smallest version: two or three seated idle animations (sipping, napping on a charpai, on the phone) and two more traveller types (a trucker, a family). r2 §5 (cosy players notice staff who just stand there); r7 §3, §6; p0 §6.
- A4 (P2) **Munshi-ji reacts to the player's fixes**, in short Hinglish lines. A new dish appears on trays and tables for the next few orders. r7, implication 5; g3, implication 5; r2 #7; p0 §9 #3, #5.
- A5 (P3) **A weekend event in the same dhaba**, such as a night mela or a wedding order, with its own goal track and one permanent reward. r5 §2; r3 §4.2; r2 §4.
- A6 (P3) **Waiter levels that show**: a bigger tray at milestones. g1 #4.

CUT
- C1 (P1) **Interstitials in the soft-launch build**, so D1–D7 can be read cleanly. Bring them back later as a test, under F1's rules. r5 §5; g2 §6; r3 §6.
- C2 (P1) **The red LOST tally**, replaced by F3. g1 #1.
- C3 (P2) **Copy that could read as real money**, such as "Earn ₹…" or "a company pays us". Use "bonus" and "collect" instead. g3, implication 4: Hindi reviewers call payout bait a trap.
- C4 (standing) **Paid manager cards, and any boost that brings more travellers than there are free seats.** g2 §6; g3, quote 16; g1 #14–15.

## (f) The three biggest risks, and how to test each

**Risk 1: the twist stays unreadable.** Dhaba Rush is a logistics puzzle dressed as a numbers game, the root of "hard to understand" (p0 §4). Test: 6–8 new testers on their own phones, no coaching, screen recorded. At minute 8, ask r1's three questions: what is slowing the dhaba, what will you buy next, what will it look like after? Pass: at least three in four name seats or waiters and find the fix unaided. Also log the time from the first "food waiting" to the second waiter, and whether the seats beat fired.

**Risk 2: there is no day 2.** New content ends after 35–50 minutes and goals after 1–1.5 hours, the cliff reviews describe (p0 §5; r2 §3). Test: the same testers play freely for seven days with analytics on. Record sessions per day, opens on days 1 and 3, minutes to the last new dish, and a daily one-liner: why did you open it today? The group is too small for benchmarks, but nobody opening on day 3 is a clear signal. For context only, Lumikai calls 30% D1 and under 10% D7 too weak to scale in India (r2). Ship A1 and A2 as soon as they exist and compare.

**Risk 3: the phone breaks the session.** The game has never run on a phone; haptics, audio, touch, pinch, safe areas and cold start are unmeasured (p0 §8). Indian 1★ reviews lead with won't start, data and heat (g3). The installed base is ageing, as the 2026 memory squeeze keeps people on old 3–4 GB phones (r7, corrected). Test: first install it on a 3–4 GB Vivo, Redmi or Realme on Android 10–11 (r7, implication 1). Measure download size, cold start, heat after 20 minutes, and one full session in airplane mode. Then ask testers to report heat, stutter or data warnings, and confirm every rewarded ad paid out.
