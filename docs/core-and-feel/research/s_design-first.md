## (a) The core of the genre

A hands-off idle tycoon is a machine the player watches and tunes. Agents walk in, get served on a timer and pay. The player's only verb is tap, read a price, buy. Under that sits Pecorella's seesaw: production leads early, costs catch up, and threshold multipliers push production ahead again. So there is always something affordable and a visible next goal every few minutes (r1 §2, §8). The one real decision is which link in a bottleneck chain to fix. The best games make the binding link visible: red stats in Idle Supermarket Tycoon (IST), a door counter in Idle Restaurant Tycoon (IRT) (r4 §8, r5 §3). Each purchase changes the scene, not only a number (r2 #6, r4 §8). Above the minute loop sits a day loop: capped offline earnings, a reason to return, and a reset dressed as a new place with a multiplier (r1 §2, g2 §6). All of it is calm: no fail state, ads the player chooses, one popup per launch (r1 §8, r2 §7).

Decoration is everything else: vehicle variety, day/night, fairy lights, music, horns, Munshi-ji, dish names, chest and VIP styling. It sells the theme, but it cannot rescue a seesaw that stalls or a chain the player cannot read (r1 §8). One India caveat: decoration does not carry the loop, but it carries installs and 5★ text. Words like "real", "Indian" and "desi" appear in 75% of 4-5★ Bus Simulator India reviews against 43% of 1-3★ (r7 §2). Recognition gets players in; the core keeps them.

## (b) What players look for, ranked

1. **Ads they choose, that always pay.** Ads appear in 46.8% of 1-3★ reviews in the India corpus, against 14.8% of 4-5★ (g3). Optional ads are Eatventure's most repeated praise across four years (r5, g2).
2. **A game that starts, runs and plays offline on their phone.** The top Hindi 1-3★ complaint is "won't start or loads slowly" at 12.6%; MB and data complaints add 7.0% (g3). The most-helpful review in that corpus (+31,148) praises offline play (g3).
3. **A calm screen with one obvious next thing.** A Hindi 1★ says everything runs by itself and they understand nothing (g3). Pop-up counts recur in 1-2★ reviews (r1 §5, r2 #3).
4. **Respect for their time, with money waiting on return.** Players praise playing five minutes or an hour without losing position (r2 #2). Kolibri's best ad placement is the return-to-double (r5 §4).
5. **A place they watch grow, where upgrades change what they see** (r2 #4, #6; r4 §8). Burger Please's stove upgrade that showed no visible change is the anti-pattern (r6 §3).
6. **Recognition and charm.** Indian roads, horns, named trucks, "add my state" (r7 §2). Truck and bus sim players ask, unprompted, for a dhaba stop (r7).
7. **Fair progress with no paywall.** 7.3% of India-corpus 1-3★ reviews say pay-to-win (g3). IST's v4 manager gate is its most-hated 2026 beat (g2, r4).
8. **Always a next place or event.** Hindi players write that they finished the game and ask for updates (g3). Kolibri's MVP lost players as soon as content ended (r5 §4).
9. **Something to think about.** The optimizer minority wants honest per-minute income, the binding station and the offline cap stated (r2 §5).
10. **It never loses their stuff.** Lost saves and updates that reset veterans are 1★ causes (r2 #8, #10).

## (d) Case study lessons

**Idle Supermarket Tycoon (r4, g2)**
1. The opening is the part players still like. A 2026 2★ says the tutorial is fun and little after it is (r4 §5). Copying the opening buys one session.
2. Legibility wins. Red stats name the binding link (r4 §8 #3).
3. A move feels like a promotion when it is previewed, priced and carries a cumulative multiplier (r4 §3). In v4 the first move comes at 9-25 minutes (g2). Moves forced before a place feels finished are resented (r1 §5).
4. Each retrofitted paid layer cost rating: Evolve timers in 2023, then manager cards from loot-box packs in v4 (from Feb 2026). English India-corpus reviews since v4 average 1.77★ (g3).
5. Dhaba Rush's x2 popup with a free reward is a v4 beat; the 2020 guides describe v1-3 (g2 §6). Copy v4's structure, the previewed move, not its monetisation.

**Eatventure, with Kolibri's IRT as the closest twin (r5, g2, g1)**
1. The ad contract is the rating gap: 4.7 on Play against IST's 4.1 (r5 §5).
2. Finishable units. A Before/After renovation preview appears at 3:00, the first renovation at about 5:30, and a city takes days (g2 §2).
3. Text-light onboarding: visuals tell the story, no big tutorial (r5 §2, quoting a 2022 interview).
4. The rewarded ad is a walking Investor character, and the permanent layer (vault, gear) is earned, not bought (r5).
5. Repetition is its top complaint: the same dishes in every city (r5). IRT adds the twin's lesson. Tables, waiters and cooks as connected bottlenecks work; walkouts with no lever, bad pathing and cleaners draw 1-2★ (r5 §3, g1 §2).

**Pizza Ready (r6, r3, g1)**
1. Instant start: no main menu and no complicated tutorial (r6, as corrected).
2. Everything physical: stacks, bills, pads. An upgrade you cannot see is a wasted purchase (r6 §7).
3. Something new every 1-3 minutes for the first 12 minutes (the My Perfect Hotel timeline, r6 §3).
4. The loop already reaches India in a foreign skin: #4 by downloads in India in 2025 (r3, r7). Hindi reviewers read it as a way to learn business (r7).
5. Its Nov 2025 switch to staff-served tables drew neither praise nor backlash; players notice ads, not who carries the food (g1). The waiter twist is validated but not novel. Players quit over ad density and a content ceiling (r6).

**India local-theme hits (r7, g3)**
1. Recognition sells: horns, named trucks, real roads (r7 §2).
2. Low-end and offline friendliness earns praise; heat, lag and storage words appear in 20-35% of the local sims' reviews (r7 §1).
3. "Add my state" is the commonest request (r7 §2). Regional variety is the natural new location.
4. Wrong or shallow authenticity is mocked: medu vada in a seafood level, empty scenes, identical cities (r7 §3).
5. Ads that gate the core loop, and a Remove Ads purchase that still shows ads, draw 1★ (r7 §3).

## (e) Dhaba Rush

**Core promise.** You own a highway dhaba that runs itself: travellers park, sit on charpais, eat and pay while you decide what to fix next. Every rupee you spend shows up in the world, until a chai counter becomes the busiest stop on the highway.

### What the seats-and-waiters twist does to the core

**Bottleneck chain.** The reference binds on department and checkout. Dhaba Rush has five links: seats, cooks, waiters, gallas and the arrival ramp. The ramp sets arrivals to the slowest of the other four (p0 §4). That is a real chain, and the chain is the genre's one real decision (r1 §8). It is also five places to get confused, which is the root of "hard to understand" (p0 §7).

**Legibility.** The chip turns the chain into one sentence, and it works (p0 §9). The world does not show the chain yet. The arrival ramp is off-screen. Waiter hires happen in a full-screen panel. The "what's slowing us" rows appear only after the kadai (p0 §9 #2, #4).

**Money source.** Throughput is hard-capped: 3 waiters, 5 tables, 2 gallas, arrivals at 19.5/min. Measured throughput plateaus near 17/min once the kadai lands (p0 §4). So the twist is the decision for roughly minutes 3-15, then a ceiling. After that, growth comes from LEVEL UP price, the least visible lever (p0 §9 #2). The twist sets the ceiling; price sets the growth.

**Verdict: keep it, change it.** Without it, Dhaba Rush is a reskin, and AppMagic and IGDA warn that copiers underestimate this genre's design (r4 §6). With it, the game has IRT's connected-bottleneck shape, which players accept when every bottleneck has a lever (g1 §2). Change four things. Make waiting lossless (g1). Show capacity in the world, not in panels (g1, r6). Add no more links: no cleaners, wages or quitting (g1 §2). And reopen the puzzle at each reset with a new layout, so the twist matters across the day loop and not only in minutes 3-15 (r1 §8, p0 §4).

### Feel targets, as observable moments

1. Within 30 s of launch a lorry honks into a bay and a cook is visibly working (r7 §7).
2. Hire a waiter: within 3 s he takes the oldest plate off the pass and "/min" ticks up (g1 #6).
3. Buy a table: the door line sits on it within 2 s, and a gate board flips to "Seats 7/8" (g1 #2, #6).
4. A full pass looks full: stacked plates, readable without a number (r6 #4).
5. In the first 30 minutes the next affordable thing is never more than about 5 minutes away (r1 §8).
6. A dish unlock changes the counter and brings a new kind of traveller within 30 s (r2 #6, p0 §9 #5).
7. At minute 8 a tester can say what is slowing the dhaba, what they will buy next and what it will look like after (r1 §8).
8. Returning after 30+ minutes shows one screen, COLLECT, and nothing else (r2 #3, g3).
9. In sessions 1-3 the tester sees no ad they did not choose (r2 #1, r5).

### KEEP / FIX / ADD / CUT

Overall order: FIX 1-5 before any ADD. Within each group, items are in priority order.

**FIX**
1. **Run it on a phone.** Smallest: one 3-4 GB Redmi, Vivo or Realme on Android 10-11; a 20-minute session; record cold start, frame time, heat, APK size and an offline launch. Aim for 150 MB or less (r7 §7, g3). The RAM split is Newzoo 2021; the 2026 memory squeeze means people keep older 3-4 GB phones longer (r7, as corrected).
2. **Interstitial timing.** Today they fire 0.6 s after an upgrade from session 4 (p0 §3). Smallest: off for soft launch behind a remote flag. If on: never within 60 s of a purchase, hire, reward or return screen; 3-5 minutes apart; 30 s or less; rummy and dating categories blocked (r6 §8, g3, r5).
3. **Walkouts.** Smallest: drop the 8 s LOST. Let the door line of 4 wait, and let vehicles drive past when it is full. Show "+₹X/min with one more table" instead of a red tally (g1 #1).
4. **Force the seats beat.** It fires in 3 of 6 seeds (p0 §8). Smallest: always inject arrivals (g1 #7; r5 §3 on Kolibri's stuck tutorial step).
5. **Show capacity in the world.** Smallest: a gate board "Seats 6/8"; plates stacking on the pass; on the waiter hire card, "One waiter ≈ 9 orders/min. You need ~12." After a STAFF hire, close the panel and show him pop in at the pass (g1 #2, #3, #6; p0 §9 #2).
6. **The economy seam.** LEVEL UP goes flat right after the lesson (p0 §9 #1). Smallest: ramp the step from the tutorial curve into the free-play curve over Lv 10-25 instead of switching (r1 §2, r4 §9 #1).
7. **Purchases that earn about +0.** SPEED or a cook off the bottleneck shows +0 (p0 §4). Smallest: label it "Not the bottleneck now" beside the honest number (r2 §5).
8. **Rewarded ads always pay.** Smallest: grant the reward if the ad fails, and hide the bubble when there is no fill (g3, r2 complaint 2).

**KEEP**
1. The first three minutes and the NEXT/PROBLEM chip (p0 §9, r4 §10).
2. Honest "/min" and before → after on every hire (p0 §9, r2 §5).
3. Seats and waiters, changed as above (r1 §5, r5 §3, g1).
4. FREE CASH in session 1 and VIP once a session from session 2 (r2 #1, g3).
5. Offline earnings, with the 2-hour cap stated on the WELCOME BACK screen (g2 §6, r2 §5).
6. The recognition layer: horns, lorries in bays, charpais, tandoor, Munshi-ji in Hinglish (r7 §2, §7).
7. The juice, with timers and the chip kept plain and readable, since juice lifts ratings but not understanding (r6 §6).

**ADD**
1. **A day loop: renovate first, move later.** Smallest for 15 Dec: a locked, priced RENOVATE card in BUILD from about minute 10, with a Before/After picture (g2 §6, r5). When the content runs out (35-50 min), the player may renovate. Levels, staff and tables reset. A cumulative income multiplier and a new look (an AC family hall, a bigger board) stay. The family hall adds seats far from the pass, so waiters matter again (r4 §3; r7 §6 notes real dhabas added AC halls). Offer it, never force it (r1 §5). After launch, a second highway in a new state with its own menu (r7 §7, r5 §5).
2. **A 24-hour return hook.** Smallest: a named regular trucker who comes once a day and pays a big bill (g2 §6; p0 §9 #3 notes no regulars; r7 §6 notes owners feed regulars).
3. **A bottleneck readout from minute one.** Smallest: four rows (seats, cooks, waiters, gallas), green or red, before the kadai (r4 §8, g1 #2).
4. **A Remove Ads price below VIP.** Smallest: Remove Ads at ₹99-199, VIP x2 at ₹299, both labelled "one-time, forever", no further offers to buyers (r7 §7, g3).
5. **A weekly event, after soft-launch D1 data.** Smallest: a weekend wedding-party bus, sized to free seats, paying a bonus (r3 §4, r5; g3 on rushes that exceed capacity).
6. **Bustle.** Smallest: two more traveller types tied to unlocks (r7 §6 traps; p0 §6 thin spots).

**CUT**
1. The interstitial 0.6 s after an upgrade (r6, g3).
2. The red LOST tally (g1).
3. Any plan for manager card packs or paid gates (r4, g2, as corrected).
4. New chain links: cleaners, wages, staff quitting (g1).
5. Bare Lv 150-300 milestone bumps as goals, once RENOVATE exists (p0 §5, r1 §4).

## (f) The three biggest risks, and how to test each with soft-launch testers

**1. It fails on the phone.** The game has never run on one (p0 §8). For Indian players, failing to start or load is the top Hindi complaint (g3), and heat, lag and storage fill 20-35% of local-sim reviews (r7). *Test:* do FIX 1 before any tester sees it. Then ask each tester for phone model and RAM. Log time from icon tap to first sale. After 20 minutes, ask whether the phone got hot or lagged. Run one session in airplane mode. *Pass (proposed):* every tester reaches the first sale, nobody reports heat, and the game launches offline.

**2. The twist is still hard to read.** The 21 Oct testers said it was hard to understand, and every fix since was verified by headless bots only (p0 §7). Kolibri found real players stuck at one early step (r5 §3). *Test:* 5-8 new testers, screen-recorded, no help. At minute 8, ask r1's three questions: what is slowing the dhaba, what will you buy next, what will it look like after. Mark every pause over 10 s and where it happened. Note whether the seats beat fired. *Pass (proposed):* most testers name the real bottleneck and the next buy. Repeat after FIX 3-5.

**3. The day cliff.** New content ends at 35-50 minutes and goals at about 1-1.5 hours, with no reset (p0 §5). Kolibri's MVP lost players when content ended (r5 §4), and IST now moves players at 9-25 minutes (g2). *Test:* let testers play freely for three days with analytics consent. Log sessions per day, the minute each session ends, and D1 and D3 returns. On day 3, ask why they last opened it and why they stopped. If RENOVATE ships, log who takes it and who returns afterwards. With a small pool, treat numbers as directions only. Reference points: top-10% idle D1 was 45.6% in GameAnalytics' 2020 study (r3), and Lumikai says 30% D1 with under 10% D7 is not enough to scale in India (r2).
